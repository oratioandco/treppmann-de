Situation

Where AI gets stuck in mid-sized companies.

The bottleneck is rarely the model or the tool. What is usually missing is a clear priority, a better process, and someone who brings leadership, business teams, and IT together.

Lots of access, little use

Copilot or a company GPT has been rolled out, but only a handful of enthusiasts use it. There are no concrete use cases in daily work, no support, and too few leaders going first.

Many ideas, no priority

Every department tries something different. Nobody decides which process changes first or how its value is measured.

A core process is stuck

Domain knowledge, exceptions, and handoffs cannot be mapped in off-the-shelf software. The team needs a better process—and a tool that fits it.

Recognize yourself? Then you are not missing technology—you are missing someone who takes ownership of it.

The first 90 days

People first: five steps.

Many AI projects start with a list of use cases. I start with how the work really runs today. The first cycle usually takes about 90 days, depending on approvals in your organisation. Then at least one AI-supported process is running that your people actually use, with a measured result—and the next one follows, with what the team has learned. IT security, legal, data protection, and architecture join as the risk requires, with specialist partners where needed.

  1. Step 1

    Set the goal

    Conversations with leadership: what should get better, and where are we today? Outcome: goals, baseline, and measures, in writing.

  2. Step 2

    Understand the processes

    With the people who run them every day: workflows, handoffs and exceptions, blockers and friction, good and bad moments. Outcome: a clear picture of where it really gets stuck.

  3. Step 3

    Choose where to start

    Rating by value, feasibility, and risk. Outcome: three initiatives in a fixed order, and a list of what we deliberately will not do. Not every answer needs AI: at ChurchDesk, days of phone tag became a booking capability for every customer, without any AI.

  4. Step 4

    Design and build

    One important process is redesigned with the people involved and built as a working solution. Plus two quick improvements people notice in daily work right away.

  5. Step 5

    Enable and measure

    Guidance on real work, AI champions with real time allocated, an AI guideline, AI literacy under Article 4 of the EU AI Act. At the end: a measured result and the next initiative.

Questions

Frequently asked questions.

Why someone with a product and design background?

Because AI rollouts rarely fail on the model—they fail on adoption. Shaping processes that people actually want to use has been my work for more than 15 years. Many AI leads today come from product management, process management, or business functions, not from research.

Why not a large consultancy?

I build myself, work directly with your teams, and hand over to your people. For specialist topics such as legal, IT security, or complex data architecture, I bring in the right partners.

How do you handle the works council and data protection?

Early and openly. The works council, data protection, and IT security are involved from the start; a clear AI guideline and firm data rules are among the first outcomes. Where AI prepares texts or decisions, a person always gives the final approval.

Do you know our industry?

Your people bring the domain knowledge; I bring the pattern. Complaints, quotes, or order handling—these are processes full of expertise, exceptions, and handoffs. I have redesigned exactly such processes at a media company, on software platforms, and in a listed financial group.

It depends on the format. The intro call is free. After it, you receive a concrete proposal with scope, outcome, and price.

Interim or permanent?

Both are possible. A good path is often to start with an interim mandate and decide after 90 days whether and how to fill the role permanently—with me if you like.
