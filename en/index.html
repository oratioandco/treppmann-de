Situation

The license isn’t the problem.

Copilot is paid for, the AI chat is set up. And yet quotes, replies, and reports get written the way they were two years ago. That’s rarely the technology. What’s usually missing is three things: a clear priority, a better workflow, and someone who gets leadership, the business team, and IT around one table.

Lots of access, little use

A few enthusiasts try everything. Everyone else works the way they always have. What’s missing: tasks from the real working day, someone alongside while people learn, and leaders who go first.

Many ideas, no order

Every department tries something different. Nobody decides which process changes first or how you’d know it worked. That’s how you get ten pilots and no result.

A core process is stuck

Domain knowledge, exceptions, and handoffs don’t fit any off-the-shelf software. The team needs a better workflow first, then a tool that fits it. In that order.

Sound familiar? Then you’re not missing technology. You’re missing someone who takes responsibility.

The first 90 days

Understand first, then build: five steps.

Most AI projects start with a list of ideas. I start with the people who do the work. It’s slower at the beginning and faster at the end. The first cycle usually takes about 90 days, depending on approvals in your organisation. After that, your people use at least one process with AI every day, and the result is measured. IT security, legal, and data protection join as the risk requires, with specialist partners where needed.

  1. Step 1

    Set the goal

    Handle more work with the same team, reply faster, or reduce rework: what should get better, and how good is it today? We settle that with leadership before choosing a tool. Outcome: goals, baseline, and measures, in writing.

  2. Step 2

    Understand the work

    We walk through a real task with your team: who does what, using which information? Where do people wait, ask follow-up questions, or redo work? We capture exceptions and handoffs too. Outcome: a clear process map that helps us identify the bottlenecks together.

  3. Step 3

    Choose where to start

    Using the process map, we assess where AI helps, where simpler automation is enough, and where human judgement is needed. Every initiative has to pass three questions: Is it worth it? Can it be done? What could go wrong? Outcome: three initiatives in a fixed order, and a list of what we deliberately won’t do. Not every answer needs AI. At ChurchDesk, days of phone tag became a booking capability for every customer, without any AI.

  4. Step 4

    Design and build

    With the people involved, I redesign one important process and build the solution so your team can still run it themselves a year from now. Plus two quick improvements people notice right away.

  5. Step 5

    Guide and measure

    I guide your people on their own tasks, not in a classroom. AI champions get real time for it. Plus an AI guideline and the AI-literacy training Article 4 of the EU AI Act requires. At the end: a measured result and the next initiative.

Questions

What you probably want to ask.

Why someone from product and design?

Because AI rollouts hinge on adoption, not technology. Making sure people actually like using software has always been my work: at Datameer, Ninox, ChurchDesk, and Bibel TV.

Why not a large consultancy?

Because what should be left at the end is a process that runs, not a slide deck. I build myself, work directly with your teams, and hand over to your people. For legal, IT security, or complex data architecture, I bring in the right partners.

How do you handle the works council and data protection?

Early and openly. The works council, data protection, and IT security have a seat at the table from the start. A clear AI guideline and firm data rules are among the first outcomes. And where AI prepares texts or decisions, a person gives the final approval.

Who owns what you build?

You do. While I work, I also use my own tools and accounts. The finished system, its documentation, and all access are handed over to you, and it runs on your infrastructure.

How much effort is this for us?

For the AI assessment: about two hours with leadership and about one hour per interviewee. In an interim mandate, I need one responsible person in the business unit with about four hours a week. I take care of the rest.

Do you know our industry?

Your people know your field. I know this kind of process: complaints, quotes, order handling—all full of expertise, exceptions, and handoffs. I’ve redesigned processes like these at a media company, on software platforms, and in a listed financial group.

What does it cost?

The intro call is free. The AI assessment has a fixed price. For everything else, you get a proposal with scope, outcome, and price.

Interim or permanent?

Both are possible. A good path is often to start with an interim mandate and decide after 90 days whether and how to fill the role permanently—with me if you like.
